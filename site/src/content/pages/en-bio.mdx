I'm Manu. I studied telecommunications engineering, specialised in electronics, and my first job, in 2015, was on a test bench in Seville, validating silicon: new imaging chips, one at a time. The bench was slow, so I automated it in Python and gave the team back more than 15 hours a week. Automating my way out of the manual part has been a habit ever since.

In Grenoble I moved into the camera itself: digital logic in VHDL and firmware in C for vision cameras that had to answer in under 10 ms. Then, in 2018, Shenzhen, as the engineer between our European teams and our customers in China. That is where I learned the part nobody teaches: a chip that works on your bench is not a product until it works in the customer's system. The industry calls that job field application engineering, and it is still the job I do.

From 2019, working from Spain with customers in Spain and the US, that job had a bottleneck. Every new camera meant weeks of integration before a customer could even try it. So I built a platform on Kubernetes and AWS that turned out an SDK and a proof of concept per camera. Onboarding a customer went from 120 days to 20–30, and the infrastructure bill fell by 80%.

Today the test bench is a cluster I run myself, on hardware I own. You can [see it running](/lab/): what it serves, how it deploys and what it measures. My AI agents work on it, under [written rules and the checks that enforce them](/ai/). The rest of what I build is in [projects](/projects/), and what I learn along the way goes into the notes below.

I never switched worlds. I just kept moving up a layer: from silicon to firmware, from firmware to the customer's system, and from there to the cloud. Every layer taught me the same thing: the work is done when it runs in someone else's hands.

Outside work I'm part of [Cloud Native Sevilla](https://community.cncf.io/cloud-native-sevilla/) and [NaN](https://nan.builders/), a community of people building with AI, and I give the odd talk when I can. And I [row](https://remoandaluz.es/).
